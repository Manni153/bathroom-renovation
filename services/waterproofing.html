Who This Is For

Three situations that usually bring people to this page

A stain on the ceiling below

A damp patch, discolouration or bubbling paint appears on the ceiling of the apartment or room below a bathroom. In a building with a condomínio, this is also a shared-liability question worth handling properly and quickly — not just repainting over it.

Re-tiling reveals a problem

Tiles come up for a re-tiling job and the waterproofing underneath turns out to be degraded, incomplete, or — in a lot of older Algarve properties — simply never there, with tiles laid directly onto the substrate.

Villa owners building a wet room or a bathroom against an exterior wall who want the tanking done right the first time, rather than finding out five years later that it wasn't.

What's Actually Involved

What proper waterproofing means in practice

Waterproofing is a system, not a single coat of paint-on membrane. Most failures we see trace back to the details, not the product.

  • A liquid-applied membrane system compliant with NP EN 14891, applied across the shower floor, the bathroom floor and the walls of direct-wet zones
  • Reinforced detailing at floor-drain collars, internal corner joints and wall-floor junctions — the specific points where a membrane actually fails, far more often than the flat areas
  • A tanking upstand on walls typically 1.8–2m high within the direct shower zone, stepping down elsewhere in the room where full-height protection isn't needed
  • A membrane system confirmed compatible with the tile adhesive going over it — an incompatible pairing can debond even when the membrane itself is sound
  • 24–48 hours between coats of membrane, and full cure before tiling begins — the exact time depends on the product and site conditions, but it's never skipped to hit a schedule
Honest Guidance

When old waterproofing has already failed

Waterproofing failure is rarely obvious from inside a finished bathroom. A membrane can be cracked, missing at a corner joint, or simply never installed, and the room can still look completely normal for years — until water finds its way through the substrate and shows up somewhere else, usually as a stain on a ceiling below.

Because of that, diagnosing a failed membrane usually isn't a job on its own. In most cases it's only confirmed once tiles are already coming up for a re-tiling or renovation project, or once damage in the room below has made it clear something needs opening up. We'll always tell you honestly what we find before covering it back up with new tiling — including if the honest answer is that the original waterproofing was never installed to any standard at all, which is common in older Algarve properties.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

We use liquid-applied waterproofing membrane systems compliant with NP EN 14891 beneath ceramic and stone tiling in bathrooms. The standard matters less for the membrane itself and more for what it forces right: correct detailing at floor-drain collars, internal corner joints and wall-floor junctions — the points where almost all real-world failures start — plus a membrane system that's actually compatible with the tile adhesive going over it. A membrane that isn't detailed properly at these junctions will leak eventually, standard or not.

The most reliable early sign is a stain, discolouration or damp patch on the ceiling of the room below a bathroom — worth taking seriously and getting checked, not just repainted over. Grout that's dark or soft at the base of the shower, or tiles that sound hollow when tapped, can also point to water getting behind them. The difficult truth is that waterproofing failure often isn't visible at all until the tiles come off — a bathroom can look fine on the surface for years while water is tracking behind it.

It depends on your specific policy and building — some seguro multirriscos and condominium insurance policies cover water damage to a neighbouring or lower-floor apartment, others exclude it or limit it depending on the cause. We can't tell you what your policy covers. What we can do is document the work — photos of the failed area before repair, the membrane system used, and a written scope of works after — which is the kind of documentation insurers typically ask for when a claim is being assessed.

Standalone remedial waterproofing — re-membraning a small wet zone and reinstating the tiling over it, typically after a leak has already caused damage — runs roughly €900–€2,200 depending on the size of the area and how much tiling has to come up. Waterproofing as part of a full bathroom renovation or re-tiling job isn't priced separately — it's included in that project's quote, since the membrane work and the tiling are done as one continuous process. See our bathroom tiling and full bathroom renovation pages for that pricing.

Standalone remedial waterproofing typically takes 3–6 working days including proper cure time — that's not a step we shorten to finish faster. As part of a full renovation, waterproofing is scheduled into the wider project timeline alongside the tiling.

It depends on the product and site conditions, but as a general range: 24–48 hours between coats of a liquid-applied membrane, and full cure before tiling begins — which can be a further 24–72 hours depending on temperature, humidity and the number of coats applied. Tiling over a membrane before it's fully cured is one of the more common causes of early failure, so we don't compress this timeline to hit a deadline.
