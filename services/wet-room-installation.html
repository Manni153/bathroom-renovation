Fully tanked, level-access wet room installation across the Algarve

A whole-room waterproofed floor, formed to fall to a drain, with no step, tray or threshold anywhere in the room. It's a bigger structural job than a shower conversion — done properly, with the floor structure checked before we quote, not after.

Who This Is For

Three situations that usually bring people to a wet room

Planning for long-term accessibility

No step, no tray lip, no threshold to catch a foot or a wheel — a wet room is the most robust way to future-proof a bathroom for ageing in place, without it needing to look institutional.

Wanting a completely level, minimalist room

Some homeowners simply want the shower-anywhere look — a single continuous floor, no glass box in the middle of the room, no visible transition between "shower area" and "bathroom".

A ground-floor step-down threshold

Older Algarve properties sometimes have a stepped-down or sunken bathroom floor. Forming a wet room floor with a proper fall to drain can solve the step-down and the waterproofing in one job.

Comparison

Wet room vs. walk-in shower — which is right for you?

Both give you a step-free shower. They differ in how much of the room is rebuilt to get there, and that difference shows up in cost, timeline and what's involved on-site. This page owns the full comparison — our walk-in shower conversion page links back here for it.

Waterproofing scopeWhole room tanked — full floor and lower walls, since the whole floor sees waterShower zone and immediate splash walls tanked; rest of the room stays dry-trade

Upper-floor apartmentsA real structural constraint — slab depth and the flat below need checking, often with condomínio input, before we quoteUsually straightforward — no whole-room fall or slab work needed, so this constraint rarely applies
Screen / enclosureOptional — a true wet room can be fully screen-freeGlass screen or partition normally required to contain water within the shower footprint
Typical cost€12,000 – €22,000+Lower — see our walk-in shower conversion page for guide figures
Typical duration12–18 working days, including membrane cure timeShorter — the shower zone is a smaller area to tank, fall and cure
Best fitLong-term accessibility, a fully level minimalist room, or a ground-floor step-down threshold you want solved for goodA step-free shower without altering the wider room's floor structure or budget

Honest summary: a wet room is a bigger structural undertaking than a walk-in shower conversion. If a level-access shower is the goal and your floor build-up or upper-floor apartment makes a full wet room impractical, a walk-in shower conversion may get you most of the day-to-day benefit for less cost and disruption — we'll say so on the site visit rather than defaulting to the bigger job.

What's Included

Everything in a wet room installation

  • Strip-out of the existing bathroom, including floor structure where the fall needs forming
  • Forming the floor falls across the whole room to a linear or point drain
  • Whole-room tanking to NP EN 14891 practice — floor and walls, not just the shower zone
  • Drain installation and collar detailing, with proper membrane cure time before tiling
  • Floor and wall tiling sized and set to work with the formed falls
  • Glass screen or partition, where wanted — a true wet room can also be left fully open
  • Fixtures — shower valve, drain cover, vanity, taps and sanitaryware
  • Site protection, daily clean-up, and full debris removal at completion
Cost Guidance

What a wet room installation typically costs

The single biggest cost driver is whether the floor structure needs altering to form the fall to drain. Ground-floor and villa wet rooms are usually more straightforward — and cheaper — than upper-floor apartment conversions, where slab depth and the flat below are a real constraint.

ScenarioTypical rangeTypical duration
Ground floor / villa, slab can be recessed€12,000 – €16,50012–15 working days
Upper-floor apartment, floor build-up raised€16,500 – €22,000+15–18 working days

Upper-floor figures assume the floor build-up can be raised without exceeding what the door threshold and ceiling height below can absorb — confirmed on-site, and coordinated with your condomínio where the works affect shared structure. Renovation labour on residential properties over two years old may also qualify for Portugal's reduced 6% IVA rate instead of the standard 23% — we confirm eligibility as part of your quote.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

A genuine wet room needs the entire bathroom floor — not just a shower tray footprint — to fall gently toward a linear or point drain, with continuous waterproofing (tanking) across the whole floor and the lower section of every wall, not just the area behind the shower screen. Forming that fall usually means altering the floor build-up: either recessing the slab before the room is built, or building the floor up around a pre-formed drainage former, which raises the finished floor level slightly. This is the main thing that separates a wet room from a walk-in shower conversion, and it's the part that needs checking on-site before any quote is final.

It can work in an apartment, but honestly it needs more upfront checking than a ground-floor or villa installation. Recessing a slab to form the drainage fall isn't usually possible in an upper-floor apartment, so the floor build-up typically has to be raised instead — and raising it has knock-on effects: it can create a step at the doorway you're trying to remove, and depending on how much build-up is needed it can be a real constraint on ceiling height in the apartment below. Before we quote an upper-floor wet room we check slab depth and existing floor levels on-site, and where the build-up is significant we recommend raising it with your condomínio, since works affecting shared structure or risers can be a legitimate shared-building concern. None of this is a reason to rule a wet room out — it's just why we won't quote one from photos alone.

Whole-room tanking to NP EN 14891 practice — liquid-applied waterproofing membrane across the entire floor and up the walls to at least 1.8–2m in the wet zone, continuing at a lower height around the rest of the room, with reinforced detailing at the drain collar, corner joints and any pipe penetrations. In a wet room this membrane is doing more work than in a standard shower conversion, because the whole floor sees standing water during use, not just a contained tray area — so detailing at the drain and floor-wall junctions gets particular attention.

As a regional guide, wet room installation typically runs €12,000–€22,000+. Ground-floor bathrooms and villas where the slab can be recessed or the floor build-up adjusted without affecting a floor below tend to sit toward the lower end. Upper-floor apartment conversions, where slab depth is limited and the flat below is a real constraint, tend to sit toward the higher end once additional structural work, engineering input or condomínio coordination is factored in. Every job gets a written, itemised quote after an on-site check of the existing floor structure — these are guide figures, not a substitute for one.

Typically 12–18 working days, including membrane cure time. That range covers strip-out, forming the floor falls and drainage, whole-room tanking with proper cure time before tiling (this is not a step we compress to hit a deadline), tiling, and fitting of the drain cover, any glass screen, and fixtures. Jobs needing floor build-up changes or slab work sit toward the longer end of that range.

A walk-in shower conversion tanks and falls a defined shower zone within the existing floor level — it's a smaller, generally faster and cheaper job. A wet room tanks and falls the entire room, is a bigger structural undertaking, and typically costs more and takes longer, but gives you a completely level, screen-optional room with no step or tray anywhere.
