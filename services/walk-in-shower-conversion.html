Who This Is For

Three situations that usually bring people to us

The bath that's never used

It's become a shelf for towels and laundry rather than something anyone actually bathes in — you'd rather have the floor space and a shower you'll use every day.

An ageing shower tray with a step

Stepping over a raised tray edge is getting harder to manage. A low-threshold or tiled-in level entry removes the step without rebuilding the whole room.

A hotel-style look before selling or a big family visit

A glass-screened walk-in shower reads as current and well cared for — useful before a valuation, a sale, or hosting family for an extended stay.

What's Included

Everything between strip-out and final fix

This is a lighter-touch conversion than a full wet room. A walk-in shower doesn't always require the same drainage rerouting a full wet room does — it waterproofs and tiles the shower zone on a tray or tiled-in base, rather than re-falling the whole room's floor to a single drain. If you want the entire room tanked and level throughout, see our wet room installation page instead.

  • Removal and disposal of the existing bath or old shower cubicle
  • Adjustment of waste and supply pipework within the existing footprint
  • Waterproofing (tanking) of the shower zone to NP EN 14891 practice
  • Choice of glass screen — fixed panel, pivot door or sliding door — or a framed enclosure
  • Wall and floor tiling of the shower zone, with make-good of surrounding tiling
  • Fitting a low-threshold shower tray, or a tiled-in level-entry base
  • Site protection, daily clean-up and full debris removal at completion

Walk-In Shower vs. Like-For-Like Replacement

Is a walk-in conversion worth it, or should you just replace what's there?

If your existing shower tray and enclosure are sound and simply dated, a like-for-like swap — new tray, new screen, re-tiled — is usually the cheaper and faster route, and sits at the lower end of the ranges above. A walk-in conversion is worth the extra cost when the current tray has a step you want gone, when the enclosure is genuinely failing (cracked seals, persistent leaks at the tray edge), or when you're converting a bath that's no longer used into shower space. Neither option re-falls the whole room's floor the way a wet room does — see our full wet room vs. walk-in shower comparison if you're weighing that bigger step instead.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Removal and disposal of the existing bath or shower cubicle, adjustment of waste and supply pipework within the existing footprint, waterproofing (tanking) of the shower zone to NP EN 14891 practice, a low-threshold shower tray or tiled-in level-entry base, a glass screen or enclosure, and tiling of the shower zone with make-good of surrounding walls. Electrical work such as adding a shaver socket or repositioning a light is coordinated with our certified electrician partner where it's needed.

As a regional guide: converting a bath to a shower on the same footprint, with waste and supply unchanged, typically runs €3,200–€4,800. Replacing an old shower cubicle with a new low-threshold walk-in shower on the same footprint runs €3,600–€5,200. A conversion that changes the layout or moves the drain point — for example switching which wall the shower sits against — runs €5,200–€7,500. These are guide figures; every job gets a written, itemised quote before work starts.

Most walk-in shower conversions take 4–7 working days, including the waterproofing cure time needed before tiling starts. A like-for-like bath-to-shower swap is usually at the shorter end of that range, around 4–5 days; a conversion that moves the drain point or waste run typically takes 6–7 days.

A conversion that stays within the existing footprint — using the same waste and supply points as before — is a non-structural change and generally doesn't need a municipal building licence. If your conversion involves moving the drain point through a shared riser, which is more common in apartment buildings, or touches a load-bearing element, we'll flag this during the site visit and advise on the comunicação prévia process with the local Câmara Municipal, plus give you a written scope of works to share with your condomínio administration before work starts.

A walk-in shower conversion waterproofs and tiles the shower zone itself, on a low-threshold or tiled-in tray, without changing how the rest of the room drains. A wet room installation tanks the entire floor and walls of the room and re-falls the whole floor to a single drain point, so the full room can safely get wet with no tray or step at all. A walk-in shower conversion is the right fit when you want a hotel-style look without rebuilding the rest of the room's construction; a wet room is the right fit for a genuinely level, trayless floor throughout the space.

We waterproof the shower zone using liquid-applied membranes compliant with NP EN 14891 beneath the tray upstand, walls and floor tiling, with particular attention to the tray-to-wall junction and any corner joints, since that's where a membrane is most likely to fail if it's rushed. Screen options include a fixed glass panel, a pivot or hinged door, or a sliding door for tighter spaces, in framed or frameless finishes. Tiling can be porcelain, ceramic or natural stone, matched to the rest of the bathroom or used to contrast with it.
