Who This Is For

Three situations that usually bring people to us

Grout has gone black or is falling out

Grout that's cracked, crumbling, or gone dark with mould despite regular cleaning is a genuine giveaway — not scare talk — that the waterproofing membrane beneath is likely compromised too. Worth checking properly before simply re-grouting over it.

Wanting a more contemporary look

Small, busy tile patterns from a previous decade replaced with large-format porcelain or natural stone for a cleaner, more current finish — without necessarily touching the layout, plumbing or fixtures.

Tiling on its own, or inside a bigger job

This page covers tiling as a standalone job. If you're also changing the layout, fixtures or plumbing at the same time, see our full bathroom renovation page — tiling is scheduled inside that project rather than quoted separately.

The Process

What actually happens before a single tile goes down

Tiling that lasts is mostly decided before the tiling starts.

Strip-out & assessment

Old tiles removed, substrate inspected. We check whether existing waterproofing (if any) is sound, cracked, or simply unknown and untrustworthy.

Substrate preparation

Levelling and repair of the base — critical for large-format tiles, which need a genuinely flat substrate to avoid lippage and cracking later.

Waterproofing membrane

A liquid-applied membrane compliant with NP EN 14891 in wet zones — floor, shower area and walls in direct splash range. Not optional if you want the tiling to last, and never skipped to save a day.

Tiling, grouting & sealing

Tile fixed with an adhesive matched to the tile type and size, grouted, and finished with silicone sealant at joints — plus sealing for natural stone.

Tile Options

What's commonly used in Algarve bathrooms

  • Standard porcelain — the most common and cost-effective choice, durable and low-maintenance, in a wide range of finishes
  • Large-format porcelain (roughly 60x120cm and up) — a cleaner, more contemporary look with fewer grout lines, but it needs a flatter substrate and a specific high-strength adhesive
  • Natural stone — limestone or marble for a distinct, high-end finish; porous, so it needs sealing at installation and periodic resealing afterwards
  • Mosaic — commonly used for shower floors, where small tiles allow the fall needed for drainage, and as feature strips or borders

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Waterproofing prep is included in every re-tiling job we quote in a wet zone — shower area, bath surround and floor. Tiling straight over old, cracked or unknown waterproofing is a false economy: the new tiles will look fine on day one, but if the membrane underneath has already failed, water gets behind the new tiling too and you're paying to redo the same job again in a few years, this time with more damage to strip out. Where the existing substrate and membrane are genuinely sound — which we check, not assume — we'll say so and won't charge you to redo work that doesn't need it.

Standard porcelain tiling (typically 30x30cm to 60x60cm) is the most common choice and the most cost-effective — durable, low-maintenance, wide range of finishes. Large-format porcelain (slabs from roughly 60x120cm up) gives a more contemporary, fewer-grout-line look but needs a flatter substrate and specific high-strength adhesive to prevent lippage and cracking, which adds labour cost. Natural stone — limestone or marble — gives a distinct, high-end finish but is porous and needs sealing at installation and periodic resealing afterwards to resist staining and etching. Mosaic tiling is commonly used for shower floors (for slope and drainage) and as feature strips or borders. We'll talk through what suits your bathroom and budget during the site visit.

To tile properly under and around sanitaryware — rather than butting tiles up against fixtures left in place, which traps old grout lines and weak points — we disconnect and remove the toilet, basin pedestal, and shower tray or bath panel, tile the full area including waterproofing detailing at the drain and corners, then refit the same fixtures (or new ones, if you're upgrading them at the same time) with fresh sealant. This is part of a standard re-tiling quote, not an extra we add on afterwards.

Typically 5–9 working days for a standalone tiling job in a mid-size bathroom, depending on the area covered, tile format and how much of that time is waterproofing membrane cure time — which we don't compress to hit a deadline. A larger bathroom, large-format tiles needing a levelling screed, or natural stone requiring sealing before grout can add a few days. If tiling is part of a wider full bathroom renovation, it's scheduled inside that overall project timeline rather than as a separate job.

As a regional guide: standard porcelain tiling for a mid-size bathroom (roughly 5–6m² of combined wall and floor coverage), including substrate prep and waterproofing, typically runs €2,800–€4,800. Large-format porcelain or natural stone for the same area typically runs €4,500–€8,000+, reflecting higher material cost, slower fixing, and — for stone — sealing. Cost scales with the area covered and how much of the existing surface needs stripping back first. Every job gets a written, itemised quote before work starts.

Porcelain tile and grout themselves need little more than regular cleaning. The parts that do need attention on a schedule are the silicone sealant joints — around the shower tray, bath and where tiling meets a different surface — which typically need replacing every 2–3 years in a well-ventilated bathroom, sooner in a poorly ventilated one where mould forms faster. Natural stone also needs resealing periodically, typically every 12–24 months depending on the stone and how much it's used, to keep it resistant to staining. Grout gone dark or patchy well before that point is usually a sign of a ventilation or sealing issue worth addressing, not just a cosmetic one.
