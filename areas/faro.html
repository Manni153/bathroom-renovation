Faro

Bathroom renovation in Faro, built around how the city is actually built

Faro's Buildings

Three property types, three different renovation contexts

City centre & Montenegro apartments

The most common context we work in — mid-rise apartment blocks from the 1970s–90s with a shared vertical riser, original plumbing runs and tiling that's often due for replacement rather than just cosmetic updating.

Vila Adentro townhouses

Faro's walled old town has narrower streets and conservation-sensitive façades. Access for materials and any skip placement is planned in advance, and we treat existing tiled or stone surfaces with extra care during strip-out.

Newer builds, airport & Ria Formosa side

More recent developments toward the airport and the Ria Formosa side generally have modern plumbing and layouts, so renovations here are more often about finish upgrades than fixing what's underneath.

Who We Work With

A city with residents, not just visitors

Faro has a large year-round population — Portuguese residents, staff and students at the Universidade do Algarve, and a growing number of English-speaking remote workers and retirees who choose Faro specifically because it isn't touristy and prices are more reasonable than the coastal resort towns. That mix shows up in the work: fewer villa-scale wet room conversions, more apartment bathrooms that need honest plumbing and waterproofing attention behind dated tiling.

Because Faro buyers are typically choosing the city for value and everyday liveability rather than a resort lifestyle, budget consciousness tends to shape the brief more here than in the coastal towns — most owners want a bathroom that looks clean, current and well-finished without paying for imported stone or custom joinery they'd get more use out of in a holiday villa. That's not a limitation on quality; it just means the emphasis is usually on doing the fundamentals properly — sound waterproofing, durable porcelain, fittings that will hold up to daily use — rather than a showpiece finish.

Local Considerations
  • Apartment blocks generally have condomínio rules on permitted working hours, noise and any shut-off affecting the shared riser — we provide a written scope of works for your building administration ahead of time.
  • Original 1970s–90s plumbing is often galvanised steel supply pipework with ageing waste connections — we assess condition during strip-out and replace what's failing rather than tiling over it.
  • A lot of older Faro bathrooms were tiled directly onto the substrate with no waterproofing membrane underneath — a common, hidden cause of damp reaching a downstairs neighbour's ceiling.
  • Faro isn't paced around a holiday-let season, so work can generally be scheduled at any time of year, not squeezed into shoulder months around summer lettings.

In practice, a typical Faro apartment renovation runs a fairly predictable course: the site visit usually turns up at least one thing worth flagging behind the old tiling — most often a missing membrane rather than a plumbing problem — and we build a contingency line into the quote for that rather than treating it as a surprise later. Lift access and permitted delivery windows are agreed with the building administration before a start date is confirmed, and because there's no seasonal rush, we can usually offer a start date within a few weeks of a written quote being approved rather than the longer lead times a resort-town job might face in peak season.

Frequently asked questions about Faro renovations

For a like-for-like bathroom renovation that stays within your unit and doesn't touch the shared waste stack or structure, you generally don't need formal condomínio approval. What Faro's mid-rise blocks do usually require is notice: most building administrations have rules on permitted working hours, noise, and any planned water shut-offs affecting the riser, since a lot of these buildings share a single vertical plumbing run across several floors. We provide a written scope of works you can hand to your administrador or gestão do condomínio before we start, and we schedule any shut-off work in the shortest possible window to minimise disruption to neighbours above and below you.

A lot of Faro's residential stock in the city centre and Montenegro dates from this period, and it's common to find original galvanised steel supply pipework, ageing waste connections, and tiling that was laid directly onto a masonry substrate with no waterproofing membrane underneath. None of that is unusual or a sign of a badly built building — it's simply the standard of the time. On site we check the condition of the exposed supply and waste runs during strip-out, replace anything showing corrosion or reduced flow, and always waterproof to current standard before retiling rather than tiling over the old surface. We flag anything more extensive — such as work that would affect the shared riser — before it's included in your quote, not after.

We apply liquid-applied waterproofing membranes compliant with NP EN 14891 beneath ceramic and stone tiling, covering the full shower zone and floor and running up walls in direct-wet areas.
