Local Context

What we typically find in Portimão bathrooms

Portimão is one of the Algarve's larger towns — a working marina, a regional hospital and shopping hub, and a mixed population of full-time residents and holiday-home owners, which means renovation demand here isn't as sharply seasonal as in some coastal resort towns. That mix shows up in the housing stock too. Immediately south of the town centre, the beachfront high-rise district of Praia da Rocha is largely built from the 1970s–90s tourism boom, and a lot of those apartment bathrooms have had no meaningful update since original construction. In the town centre itself, ordinary Portuguese apartment blocks and townhouses sit at a more affordable price point than Albufeira or Vilamoura, drawing owners who want beach proximity without resort-town pricing.

Praia da Rocha and the town centre attract different kinds of owners, and that difference shapes what a renovation brief actually looks like in each. Along the seafront tower blocks, a meaningful share of apartments belong to non-resident holiday-let owners or foreign second-home owners who visit for a few weeks a year and let the property out the rest of the time — for them, the priorities tend to be durability under heavy guest turnover, straightforward future maintenance, and fitting the work into a gap between bookings rather than a bespoke, once-in-a-lifetime finish. In the town centre, most of the properties we work in are owner-occupied by full-time residents, so the brief reads more like an ordinary family home renovation: a layout that suits daily use, a finish the owner expects to live with for years, and generally less pressure to hit a narrow calendar window. We scope the two differently from the first site visit rather than assuming one profile fits the whole town.

The single biggest issue we see in Praia da Rocha's older stock is waterproofing that was never properly there to begin with — it's common to find tiles set directly onto masonry with no real tanking membrane beneath, which only becomes obvious once strip-out starts. Before we quote a retile in one of these apartments, we treat checking the existing waterproofing as step one, not a footnote. In condomínio buildings — which covers most of the Praia da Rocha tower stock — coordinating access, lift bookings and shared-riser work with the building administration is also a real planning step we build into the schedule rather than leaving to chance.

In practice, a typical Portimão job starts the same way wherever it is: a site visit, followed by — critically, in the older Praia da Rocha stock — an honest look at what's actually happening beneath the existing tiling before we commit to a fixed scope. Because that waterproofing question usually can't be answered with certainty until strip-out begins, we build a contingency allowance into every Praia da Rocha quote for rebuilding the membrane, rather than pricing on the assumption it's fine and revising upward once the tiles come off. Once that diagnosis is done, the sequence is fairly predictable — strip-out, then a new membrane where one is needed, left to cure properly before anything is tiled over it, then tiling and fixtures — and in a tower we plan that sequence around the condomínio-agreed lift booking and working-hour windows rather than around a generic project timeline. In the town centre, where original waterproofing is less consistently a problem, the same site visit more often confirms the substrate is sound, and the job moves straight into renovation without that extra diagnostic step.

Services in Portimão

What we're most often asked to do here

Waterproofing

The priority in older Praia da Rocha towers — assessing and, where needed, rebuilding the membrane beneath tiling to NP EN 14891 practice before any new tiling goes down. Because so many of these towers share a single vertical riser, a missing membrane isn't just a risk to your own bathroom — it's the kind of hidden defect most likely to eventually show up as damp on a neighbour's ceiling below, which is why we treat it as the first thing to check rather than the last.

Full Bathroom Renovation

A complete renovation delivered as one project, for the original-condition apartments across both Praia da Rocha and the town centre. It's the natural route once a Praia da Rocha bathroom's fixtures, tiling and waterproofing are all original to the 1970s–90s build, since tackling them together avoids re-opening a newly tiled wall later for a plumbing or membrane issue found after the fact.

Bathroom Tiling

New tiling on a properly prepared, waterproofed substrate — not laid over an untested original membrane, or the lack of one. This is the more common standalone request in the town centre, where the substrate is more often sound and the job is mainly about bringing dated tiling up to current condition rather than rebuilding what's underneath it.

During the site visit we assess the existing tiling and, once strip-out begins, inspect what's actually beneath it — in a lot of Praia da Rocha's original tourism-boom stock, that turns out to be tiles set directly onto masonry with little or no real tanking underneath. Where that's the case, retiling over it is not something we'll recommend, because it just seals the same risk back in. Our standard approach for these apartments is to strip back to the substrate and apply a liquid-applied membrane compliant with NP EN 14891 before any new tiling goes down, with particular attention to the floor-drain collar and wall-floor junctions where old membranes typically failed first, if they existed at all.

Most bathroom renovations that keep the same layout and don't touch shared risers don't require a municipal licence, but in an apartment tower it's still good practice to notify the condomínio administration before works involving water shut-offs, noise, or access to shared pipework — a lot of Praia da Rocha's towers are large enough that this is a real coordination step, not a formality. We provide a written scope of works you can hand to the building administration, and we flag early if your renovation would touch a shared waste stack, since that changes the conversation with the condomínio and potentially with the Câmara Municipal.

Beyond condomínio notification, the practical side is booking the lift for material deliveries and waste removal, since most towers have only one service lift and a shared booking schedule other residents and other trades are also using. We agree delivery slots and any restricted working hours directly with the building administration before a start date is confirmed, and where a tower doesn't allow bagged waste to sit in the communal areas, we time skip or waste-bag collection to match those same booked windows rather than letting rubble build up in a corridor. In the town centre, where many properties are smaller blocks or townhouses with street-level or stair access, this kind of formal booking is rarely needed and materials can generally move in and out with just reasonable notice to close neighbours.

As a regional guide: a like-for-like renovation (same layout, mid-range fixtures and porcelain tiling) typically runs €5,500–€9,500. Where an older Praia da Rocha apartment needs the waterproofing rebuilt from the substrate up rather than a simple retile, that additional strip-out and membrane work typically adds €1,500–€3,500 depending on the size of the wet zone. A renovation with layout changes or upgraded finishes runs €9,500–€16,000. Every job gets a written, itemised quote after a site visit — these are guide figures, not a substitute for one.

The work itself is similar — a like-for-like renovation typically takes 10–14 working days, longer where waterproofing has to be rebuilt, since the membrane needs proper curing time before tiling starts. What differs in a high-rise is access: lift booking windows, building-imposed working hours, and getting materials up to a higher floor can add planning time before the on-site clock even starts, which is why we confirm building rules with the condomínio during scoping rather than after we've started.
