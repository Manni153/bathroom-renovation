Bathroom renovation in Olhão, built around flat-roofed townhouses and original plumbing

Olhão is a working fishing town on the Ria Formosa, not a resort — its old streets are built up of cube-shaped, flat-roofed Algarvian townhouses where bathrooms often haven't been touched in decades. Olhão's Buildings

Three property types, three different renovation contexts

Historic cube-shaped townhouses

The flat-roofed, whitewashed houses that define Olhão's old town, sometimes compared to North African building styles. Bathrooms here are often original, with no waterproofing membrane and an açoteia roof above that ages along with everything else.

Conventional apartment buildings

More typical mid-rise apartment blocks around the town, generally with condomínio arrangements and plumbing runs in better, more predictable condition than the historic core.

Newer developments toward Pechão & Moncarapacho

More recent builds on the edges of Olhão, where renovations are more often about finish upgrades than fixing hidden plumbing or waterproofing problems.

Who We Work With

A real, working town — not a resort

Olhão has kept a genuinely local, unpolished character compared with the coastal resort towns nearby, and that's exactly why it's attracting a newer wave of English-speaking buyers — people who want a real Portuguese town with more affordable property than Vilamoura, Albufeira or the coastal strip, not a holiday-resort feel. A lot of what we're asked to do here is bring an original bathroom in an older townhouse up to a proper modern standard while respecting the character of the house around it, alongside more routine updates in the town's apartment buildings and newer developments.

Local Considerations
  • Flat-roof (açoteia) construction and older masonry mean waterproofing and damp are a genuinely common starting point for the renovation conversation here, more so than in newer-build areas.
  • Older townhouse bathrooms frequently have no waterproofing membrane under the existing tiling at all, since that wasn't standard practice when the tiling was originally laid.
  • Narrow streets in the historic core can limit vehicle access for materials and skips — we plan smaller-vehicle drop-offs and hand-carrying in advance where needed.
  • Apartment buildings and newer developments toward Pechão and Moncarapacho generally follow condomínio notice rules rather than the access and conservation questions of the old town.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about Olhão renovations

Olhão's older streets are built up of the cube-shaped, flat-roofed houses (locally the roof terrace is an açoteia) that give the town its distinctive look. For a bathroom renovation, the flat roof matters as much as the room itself: if the bathroom sits directly under an açoteia, and that roof's own waterproofing is old or was never properly detailed at the parapet and drain outlets, water can track down into the bathroom below in a way that looks like a bathroom problem but actually starts on the roof. Before we quote a renovation in one of these houses, we check whether the roof above the bathroom needs attention as part of the job, not after it, so you're not retiling a room that leaks again from above within a year or two.

In the older townhouse core, it's common to find a bathroom that hasn't had a substantial update in decades — original galvanised steel supply pipework, ageing waste connections, and tiling laid directly onto solid masonry with no waterproofing membrane underneath at all, since that wasn't standard practice when these houses were built or last retiled. Combined with the older, thicker masonry walls typical of the area and, where relevant, an ageing flat roof above, damp is a genuinely common starting point for the renovation conversation here — more so than in Olhão's newer apartment buildings and developments toward Pechão and Moncarapacho, where the plumbing and waterproofing are usually still in reasonable condition.

A single-owner townhouse in Olhão's old streets that keeps the same layout and doesn't touch load-bearing walls or the roof structure generally doesn't need a building licence, though work affecting the façade or roofline of an older house can fall under conservation-area sensitivities the Câmara Municipal cares about, and we'll flag that before quoting if it applies. If you're in one of the more conventional apartment buildings in Olhão, or a newer development toward Pechão or Moncarapacho, formal condomínio approval usually isn't required for an in-unit bathroom renovation, but the building administration will typically want notice of any noise, water shut-offs or work affecting a shared riser — we provide a written scope of works for that purpose.

Olhão's historic core has narrow, sometimes pedestrian-only streets between the flat-roofed townhouses, and vehicle access for a skip or a full delivery isn't always possible right outside the door. Where that's the case, we plan smaller-vehicle drop-offs, hand-carrying materials the last stretch, and waste-bag rather than skip removal in advance of the job starting, so it's built into the schedule rather than a surprise on day one. Apartment buildings and the newer streets toward Pechão and Moncarapacho generally have normal vehicle access.

As a regional guide for Olhão's older townhouse stock: retiling and re-waterproofing an existing bathroom layout (no plumbing moves) typically runs €4,000–€6,800, often higher than a newer-build equivalent because more of the old substrate needs stripping back and there's usually no existing membrane to work around. A full like-for-like renovation — new suite, tiling, waterproofing and fixtures on the existing layout — typically runs €5,500–€9,500. Where the bathroom sits under an açoteia and the roof above also needs re-waterproofing, that's assessed and priced as an additional, clearly itemised scope, typically €900–€2,500 depending on the area of roof affected — never bundled invisibly into the bathroom price. Every quote is written and itemised after a site visit.

Portugal applies a reduced 6% VAT rate (instead of the standard 23%) to renovation labour on residential properties at least two years old, subject to conditions in the Portuguese VAT code — a rule that in practice covers most of Olhão's older townhouse stock, given how long many of these houses have stood. Eligibility still depends on the specific property, its use and the scope of works, so we confirm it as part of the quoting process rather than assuming it applies.
