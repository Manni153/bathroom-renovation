Aljezur, Algarve

A different kind of Algarve renovation

Aljezur sits apart from the built-up resort belt of the central and eastern Algarve. It's a west-coast town within the Costa Vicentina natural park, known for its surf beaches and a rural, laid-back character rather than golf resorts and beachfront apartment blocks. The English-speaking buyers we hear from here tend to be a specific niche — surfers, remote workers, and wellness-focused buyers drawn to smallholdings, traditional whitewashed cottages, and countryside plots rather than turnkey resort units.

What this buyer actually wants from a bathroom shapes the brief more than budget alone. Someone who moved to Aljezur for the surf, the slower pace, or a yoga-and-wellness routine built around the beach at Arrifana or Monte Clérigo is rarely chasing a marble-clad, hotel-style suite — the request we hear far more often is a bathroom that feels honest to the building: natural materials, a walk-in shower rather than a fitted bath, good ventilation for a house that's occupied year-round rather than closed up for winter, and fittings robust enough to cope with sandy feet, wetsuits rinsed off after a surf session, and salty coastal air. Showy isn't really the aim; durable, characterful and quietly well-built is. That preference also shows up in how owners talk to us at the site visit — most have already lived with the space for a while and know exactly which draughts, damp patches or awkward corners they want solved, rather than arriving with a Pinterest board of resort-bathroom photos.

That property type changes what a bathroom renovation actually involves. Two things come up honestly and often here, and we'd rather raise them at the site visit than assume them away:

  • Private well or borehole water supply — common on rural and countryside properties around Aljezur, and it affects pressure, flow rate, and which fixtures will actually perform well
  • Septic tank drainage — on older or more remote properties without mains sewage, which changes how the plumbing scope is designed rather than assumed to be standard

Scope here also tends to lean smaller and more characterful than the large luxury-villa jobs typical of Vilamoura or Quinta do Lago — a traditional cottage bathroom brought up to a modern, properly waterproofed standard while keeping the building's stone, beams and proportions intact, rather than a full-scale suite fitout.

In practice, a typical Aljezur job starts with more groundwork than a village-centre renovation would need. Alongside the usual look at layout, tiling and existing fixtures, the site visit includes checking the property's water source and drainage — is it on a private well or borehole, and what's the static pressure and flow rate; is it on mains sewage or a septic tank, and if so, roughly what condition and capacity is the system in. Neither check takes long, but skipping it is how a renovation ends up with a shower that never quite reaches proper pressure or a WC that overloads an ageing septic system. Once that's established, the rest of the job runs much like anywhere else in the Algarve — strip-out, waterproofing, tiling, fixtures — just sequenced around a delivery schedule that accounts for a longer or unpaved access track where that applies, and around any pressure pump, filtration or septic-aware drainage work identified up front rather than discovered mid-job.

Services Relevant to Aljezur Properties

What we're usually asked to do here

Bathroom Plumbing & Fixtures

The most requested service around Aljezur. We test private well or borehole pressure and flow before recommending fixtures, and design waste runs and fixture choices around septic tank drainage where that applies — rather than fitting standard mains-pressure hardware and hoping it performs. It's usually the first service we're asked about here precisely because so many Aljezur properties sit outside the reach of mains water and sewage, so getting the plumbing brief right from the outset avoids a second visit to fix an underperforming fitting later.

Full Bathroom Renovation

For a traditional cottage bathroom being brought fully up to date — a complete renovation delivered as one project, sized to the smaller, characterful scope typical of Aljezur's older buildings rather than a large villa suite. Because so many of these cottages have never had a proper renovation at all, bundling the plumbing, waterproofing and tiling into one project avoids opening the same wall or floor twice, which matters more here than in a town where the building's systems are already modern.

Waterproofing

Older cottage and smallholding construction was rarely built with a modern wet-zone membrane underneath the original tiling. We tank shower zones, floors and wet walls to NP EN 14891 practice, with extra attention to detailing where floors are uneven or walls are thick rubble-stone. Thick stone or adobe walls also hold moisture differently to modern block construction, so getting the membrane and detailing right here is what actually protects the building's fabric long-term, not just the tiling finish.

An internal bathroom renovation that doesn't alter the building's exterior, footprint or load-bearing structure generally doesn't trigger the additional planning sensitivities that apply to exterior or structural works within the Costa Vicentina natural park boundary — those restrictions are mainly aimed at extensions, new construction and facade changes. If your renovation does involve moving a wall, changing an opening, or anything visible from outside, we'll flag it and advise on the comunicação prévia process with the Câmara Municipal de Aljezur before quoting, rather than assuming it's exempt.

As a regional guide: a like-for-like renovation (same layout, mid-range fixtures) typically runs €5,500–€9,500. A renovation that changes the layout or upgrades finishes runs €9,500–€16,000. Where a private well or septic system needs additional plumbing work — a pressure pump, filtration, or septic-aware drainage sizing — that's scoped and priced separately as part of the same written quote, since it varies property to property. Rural smallholding properties can also mean a longer supply run for materials than a village-centre job, which we account for at the site visit rather than as a surprise later.

Timeframes are similar to elsewhere in the Algarve — typically 10–14 working days for a like-for-like renovation and 14–20 working days where the layout changes or well/septic-related plumbing work is involved — but we build in realistic delivery windows for materials on properties further from Aljezur's village centre or down unpaved access tracks. We'll talk through vehicle access for skips, deliveries and equipment during the site visit so the schedule we give you reflects the property, not a generic estimate.

Before we put a number on any plumbing work, we ask about the septic system directly at the site visit: roughly how old the tank is, when it was last emptied, and whether there's a visible inspection or access point we can look at. We're not there to fully assess the tank's structural condition — that's a separate specialist job if a proper inspection is needed — but what we can see helps us decide whether the design should stay conservative, for example specifying a low-flush WC and avoiding a large soaking bath that would add significant extra load. Where the tank's condition or capacity is genuinely unclear, we'll say so plainly and suggest having it checked properly before we finalise the plumbing scope, rather than guessing and hoping it holds up.
