Quarteira's Property Stock

An older, more affordable town right next to Vilamoura

Quarteira sits immediately next to Vilamoura but feels like a different town entirely. It grew up as a working fishing port and kept much of that character as tourism arrived — a lively daily market, a genuinely year-round Portuguese and international resident population living alongside holiday-let apartments, and noticeably more affordable property than its purpose-built resort neighbour. Most of the housing stock is mid-rise apartment blocks built from the 1970s through the 1990s, concentrated along and just back from the seafront, rather than the villas and gated developments you'd find in Vilamoura.

That building stock drives most of the renovation work we do here. A large number of Quarteira bathrooms have never been properly modernised — original 1980s tiling, original fittings, waterproofing (if any) from before any modern standard existed. We're also seeing gradual redevelopment as local owners and international buyers who want better value than Vilamoura pick up older apartments and renovate them, sometimes as a first step before renting or reselling.

The ownership pattern reflects that year-round character. Unlike Vilamoura's golf villas and gated developments, often owned by seasonal residents who spend a handful of months a year in the Algarve and treat the property as a lock-up-and-leave, a large proportion of Quarteira's apartment owners live in the town full time, work locally or commute to nearby Faro and Loulé, or have owned the same flat for decades as a long-standing primary or second home. Alongside them is a distinct group of international buyers — often British, Irish, Dutch, German or Scandinavian — who are deliberately choosing Quarteira over Vilamoura or Albufeira because the entry price is lower and the town feels like somewhere people actually live rather than a resort built for holidaymakers. Both groups tend to renovate for the same underlying reason — a bathroom that has never been touched since the building went up — rather than as part of a wider luxury refit, and that shapes most Quarteira briefs toward solid, durable modernisation rather than a showpiece finish.

Services We Prioritise Here

Most requested for Quarteira's apartment stock

Given the age and type of building most common in Quarteira, these are the services that come up most often.

Full Bathroom Renovation

A complete strip-out and rebuild for a bathroom that's original or close to it, delivered as one project. It's the most straightforward route for a lot of Quarteira apartments precisely because so many bathrooms here have never been renovated at all — there's no earlier work to work around, just a full-condition assessment and a clean rebuild.

Bathroom Tiling

Replacing small, dated original tiling with a properly waterproofed, modern-format finish — the most common single request we get in Quarteira apartments. Because so much of the town's stock is original, this is rarely a cosmetic swap; it almost always means addressing what's (or what isn't) beneath the old tiles as part of the same job.

Waterproofing

Where original tanking is minimal, missing, or long past its working life — addressed properly beneath new tiling to NP EN 14891 practice, not papered over. In a shared building like most of Quarteira's blocks, a failed or missing membrane is also the detail most likely to end up as a downstairs neighbour's problem, so we treat it as a priority rather than an afterthought.

Working In Apartment Blocks

Local considerations specific to Quarteira

  • Shared water risers mean a strip-out often requires a scheduled shut-off affecting more than one apartment — we plan and communicate this in advance
  • Noisy work (demolition, drilling, tile-cutting) is grouped into concentrated blocks and timed with the condomínio and neighbours in mind
  • Lift access and stairwell width vary a lot between Quarteira blocks — we confirm access for materials and waste removal during the site visit
  • Original waterproofing in unrenovated bathrooms is frequently absent or degraded, which we check for and address before new tiling goes down
  • Renovation labour on properties over two years old may qualify for Portugal's reduced 6% IVA rate — we confirm eligibility as part of the quote

In practice, a typical Quarteira job in one of these older blocks follows a fairly predictable shape once we're on site. The first day or two is strip-out — pulling the old bath, tiling and any partition back to the substrate — and this is usually when we find out whether the original waterproofing exists at all, since in a lot of buildings from this era it simply wasn't installed under the tiling in the first place. Water to the riser is shut off in short, pre-agreed windows rather than left off for the day, worked out with the condomínio or property manager so upstairs and downstairs neighbours know exactly when to expect it. Materials come up via whatever access the block actually has — a working lift in some buildings, stairs only in others — and we confirm that during the site visit rather than assuming, since it affects how deliveries are staged and how much can realistically be moved in a single day. Once the substrate is sound, waterproofing goes down and is left to cure properly before tiling starts, which is usually the point in the schedule where an older building's slower pace of neighbour coordination, rather than the work itself, sets the pace.

A large share of Quarteira's housing stock is 1970s–90s apartment blocks, many along or near the seafront, and a good number of bathrooms in them are still original or close to it. That usually means a cast acrylic or steel bath with no shower screen, small ceramic wall tiles from the original build (sometimes 1980s patterns), a floor drain and waterproofing detail that predates any modern standard, and dated single-lever or two-tap fittings. We see a lot of first renovations in these units — bathrooms that have never been properly modernised since the block was built — rather than a refresh of something already renovated once.

Most Quarteira apartments sit within a shared building where the water supply riser serves multiple units, so a bathroom strip-out usually means shutting off water to more than just your own apartment for short periods. Before work starts we put together a written scope and a proposed schedule for any shut-offs or noisy work (demolition, drilling, tiling prep) so you or your property manager can notify the condomínio and neighbours in good time. We plan louder or disruptive tasks into concentrated blocks rather than spreading them across the whole project, and we confirm timing with the building administration wherever the works affect shared risers, waste stacks or common areas.

As a regional guide for Quarteira's typical apartment stock: replacing a dated original bathroom like-for-like (same layout, mid-range fixtures, new tiling and waterproofing) usually runs €5,000–€8,500. Where the original layout needs changing — swapping a bath for a walk-in shower, moving a vanity — typical costs run €8,500–€13,000. Apartment-specific factors like limited lift access, on-site parking for materials, and coordinating with the condomínio can affect timing more than cost. Every project gets a written, itemised quote after a site visit, not an estimate over the phone.

A like-for-like renovation of an original bathroom typically takes 9–13 working days from strip-out to final fix. Where the original waterproofing and tiling need to come up fully — common when a bathroom hasn't been touched since the 1980s or 90s — we build in proper curing time for the new waterproofing membrane before tiling starts, which we don't compress to hit a deadline. Access matters too: buildings with a working lift and reasonable material access at street level generally move faster than upper-floor units without one.

A renovation that keeps the existing layout, doesn't touch load-bearing walls and doesn't reroute waste through the shared stack generally doesn't need a municipal building licence. In an apartment building it's still good practice to notify the condomínio administration ahead of any works involving noise, water shut-offs to shared risers, or access to common stairwells and lifts — we provide a written scope of works you can pass on. If your renovation does involve structural changes or a different drainage route, we'll flag that and advise on the comunicação prévia process with the Câmara Municipal before quoting.

In an original Quarteira bathroom that's 30–50 years old, it's rarely just the visible surfaces. Original waterproofing beneath the tiling, if it exists at all, is usually well past any usable lifespan, and small original tiles often hide cracked grout lines that have been letting moisture through for years — sometimes visible as staining on the ceiling of the apartment below. Supply pipework and the floor drain connection are also typically original. Because of that we treat these as full strip-out renovations rather than a surface refresh: new NP EN 14891-compliant waterproofing, new tiling, and a check of the waste and supply connections, even when the brief starts out as "just replace the tiles".
