Vale do Lobo

Bathroom renovation for Vale do Lobo's villas, townhouses and praça apartments

Vale do Lobo's clifftop setting above its two beaches and long-established golf courses comes with one of the widest mixes of property age on this stretch of coast — well-established luxury villas from the resort's earlier development decades sit alongside newer builds and the upscale apartments and townhouses around the praça. Vale do Lobo's Properties

Three renovation starting points, not one

Established luxury villas

Villas from the resort's earlier development decades, often clifftop or close to the beaches. Bathrooms here are typically due a full modern update rather than a cosmetic refresh.

Newer villa builds

More recently built villas, closer in spirit to Quinta do Lago's architect-led stock next door. Renovations here are more often about finish upgrades — tiling, vanities, fittings — on plumbing and waterproofing that's already sound.

Praça apartments & townhouses

A significant stock of upscale apartments and townhouses around Vale do Lobo's praça, generally newer or recently updated, with condominium-style building administration alongside the resort's own management.

Who We Work With

Owners who visit, not owners who live on site

Many Vale do Lobo owners are internationally based and visit seasonally rather than year-round, so a renovation usually needs to be planned, quoted, carried out and signed off without requiring you to be physically present for most of it. That shapes how we run the project as much as the property itself does: clear written scope agreed remotely, photo updates through the works, and coordination with your property manager or a local keyholder where one is in place.

That range in property age shows up just as clearly in who owns each type of bathroom, and it's worth understanding which end of the range you're at before scoping a project. Owners of Vale do Lobo's earlier-era villas are typically long-standing owners — the property was bought decades ago when the resort was newer, and the original bathroom suite has simply never been brought up to a standard that matches a since-updated kitchen or living space elsewhere in the house. For these owners, a renovation is usually the last major room to catch up, and most are comfortable going into a full strip-out because they already suspect what's likely underneath the tiling. Owners of newer-build villas and praça apartments, by contrast, are more often recent purchasers or owners who bought specifically for the more contemporary spec, and their bathroom already starts from sound plumbing and a properly waterproofed substrate — the brief for them is almost always about finish upgrades: a more current tile format, a freestanding bath, better lighting and joinery, rather than fixing anything structural behind the wall. Both groups tend to treat the bathroom as one part of a wider improvement across the property, but the starting condition — and therefore how much of the first phase of work is remedial rather than cosmetic — differs considerably between the two.

Local Considerations
  • Renovation work is generally expected to be registered with Vale do Lobo's resort estate management ahead of any municipal requirement that applies to the works themselves.
  • Villas from the resort's earlier development decades often have first-generation tiling laid without a proper waterproofing membrane, and original plumbing runs worth assessing during strip-out.
  • Praça apartments and townhouses typically involve a building administration alongside the resort's own access and contractor policies — we provide a written scope of works for both.
  • Seasonal, internationally based ownership means projects are usually planned to run while owners are away, with remote sign-off rather than requiring you on site.

In practice, a typical Vale do Lobo job starts with registering the works with the resort's estate management well before any tools arrive — confirming permitted delivery windows, contractor vehicle passes, and any restrictions on skip or waste-bag placement, since access across the resort is more tightly controlled than on a typical residential street. Because so many owners are only on site for part of the year, most of the day-to-day coordination happens through a local keyholder or the owner's property manager rather than the owner directly: they let the team in each morning, field any access queries from resort security, and act as the point of contact if a delivery needs rescheduling around the resort's own calendar. We send photo updates through the works so an owner following along from abroad can see progress without needing to be present, and on a villa that sits empty for long stretches between visits, we're careful to leave the property secure and watertight at the end of each working day rather than mid-strip-out. Final sign-off is scheduled either for the owner's next visit or handled remotely with a full photo record, whichever suits their travel pattern.

Praça apartments and townhouses are more often newer or recently updated than the resort's original villa stock, so renovations here more commonly focus on finish upgrades — tiling, fixtures, vanities — than full waterproofing rebuilds, though we assess each on its own condition rather than assuming.

We use liquid-applied waterproofing membranes compliant with NP EN 14891 beneath ceramic and stone tiling in shower zones, floors, and up walls in direct-wet areas, with particular attention to floor-drain collars and corner detailing. It matters more in villas from the resort's earlier development decades, where a bathroom retiled at some point since may have gone straight over the original surface with no membrane underneath — a common, hidden cause of damp that only shows up as staining or a soft floor years later. We check for this during strip-out rather than assuming a previous retile was done to current standard.

Timeline follows the property's starting condition more than its size. A finish-focused renovation in a newer villa or praça apartment, where the plumbing and waterproofing are already sound, typically takes 10–14 working days. A full renovation of an older-era villa bathroom, where supply and waste runs and the waterproofing membrane are being replaced alongside the finishes, typically takes 15–20 working days. A wet room conversion with structural drainage changes usually falls in a similar 15–20 day range, depending on the floor build-up required. We agree a working timeline as part of the written quote, and because so many owners are scheduling around a visit, we build in a realistic buffer rather than a best-case figure.
