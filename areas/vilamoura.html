Vilamoura Properties

A resort town with its own rhythm — and its own rules

Vilamoura is a purpose-built marina and golf resort, largely developed from the 1970s onward. Most of the work we do here falls into two property types: modern villas inside gated golf-course communities, and mid-to-high-spec apartment complexes around the marina. Both tend to be owned by a strong mix of UK, Irish and Nordic second-home buyers who expect a finish specification above the Algarve average — large-format porcelain, walk-in showers replacing under-used baths, underfloor heating — in keeping with the resort's overall market positioning.

That buyer base shapes the brief in specific ways. Many UK, Irish and Nordic owners are renovating a second home they've held for several years and are weighing our process against a renovation they've done, or are currently doing, back home — so they tend to arrive with clear reference points: named tile formats and finishes rather than vague preferences, an expectation of a written specification rather than a verbal agreement, and specific questions about how underfloor heating or a double vanity would be integrated, not whether to include them at all. Because many won't be on site for the bulk of the build, they also expect more structured progress communication than a typical local renovation — photo updates at agreed stages and a single point of contact — since checking something in person means a flight, not a quick visit next door. Nordic owners in particular often ask early about underfloor heating zoning and ventilation, having renovated bathrooms in a colder climate where those decisions are second nature; UK and Irish owners more often lead with layout and storage, coming from properties where a bathroom this size would be unusual. We treat both as a normal part of scoping the brief rather than something to talk owners out of.

What sets Vilamoura apart practically is condominium administration. Villa communities and apartment blocks here are more consistently managed by an active homeowners' association than in an ordinary Algarve apartment building, often with defined working-hour restrictions, contractor access registration, and sometimes a requirement to submit a short scope of works before internal renovation begins. We treat this as a normal, worthwhile step in a Vilamoura project rather than paperwork to work around.

The other consistent factor is occupancy. Many Vilamoura properties are used seasonally by their owners rather than let out on a rental calendar, so the main timing consideration is usually when you'll next be in Portugal — not filling gaps between guests. We plan strip-out, wet works and curing time around your travel dates.

Day to day, a typical Vilamoura job is paced by two things running in parallel: the condominium's paperwork and the calendar of your own visits. Before a start date is confirmed we typically request a copy of your community's internal rules, register site access and expected delivery times with the administration or gestão, and agree the working-hour window those rules specify — often tighter than the 8am–7pm we'd otherwise work to, particularly in higher-density marina blocks with shared corridors. Contractor vehicles and skip placement are also frequently restricted to specific bays or times within gated communities, so we confirm that in advance rather than discovering it on the first morning. Combined with scheduling wet works and curing time around your own occupancy, this means a Vilamoura project usually has a slightly longer lead-in before the first day on site than an equivalent renovation elsewhere in the Algarve, even though the on-site build time itself is comparable. Where a property is managed on your behalf by a local property manager or keyholder, we're also happy to coordinate site access and material deliveries directly with them between your visits, so the administration and scheduling side of the project doesn't depend on you being reachable in real time from overseas.

As a guide, reflecting the higher-spec finishes typical of Vilamoura properties: a like-for-like renovation with large-format porcelain and a walk-in shower runs roughly €7,000–€11,000. A renovation with layout changes or upgraded finishes (natural stone, underfloor heating, double vanity) runs €11,000–€17,000. A full wet room conversion with structural drainage changes runs €17,000–€27,000+. A standalone walk-in shower conversion, replacing a bath without wider layout changes, typically runs €4,000–€7,500. Every job gets a written, itemised quote before work starts.

Vilamoura falls under the Câmara Municipal de Loulé. A renovation that keeps the existing layout and doesn't alter load-bearing walls or shared drainage routes generally doesn't need a municipal building licence. Where a wet room conversion changes floor drainage, or a layout change affects a shared wall or riser, we flag it and advise on the comunicação prévia process with Loulé's Câmara before quoting — on top of whatever your condominium's own approval process requires.

We use liquid-applied waterproofing membranes compliant with NP EN 14891 beneath ceramic and stone tiling in shower zones, floors, and up to 1.8–2m on walls in direct-wet areas, with particular attention to floor-drain collars and corner detailing. For wet rooms and level-access walk-in showers this matters more than usual, since there's no shower tray or upstand acting as a second line of defence — the membrane and its detailing are doing the full job.

A retile-and-rewaterproof or standalone walk-in shower conversion typically takes 6–10 working days on site, and a full renovation or wet room installation typically takes 12–18 working days — slightly longer than our general Algarve guide figures, reflecting the higher-spec finishes and condominium-permitted working hours common here. Because most owners plan the work around a specific gap between visits, we'd recommend getting in touch and confirming a written quote at least 6–8 weeks ahead of your intended start date, and earlier still if you're hoping to schedule strip-out around a particular shoulder-season window when demand across the resort tends to be higher.
